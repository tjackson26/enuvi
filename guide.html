How to set up your blocklist

Enuvi Browser blocks the sites on a list. A friend you trust keeps that list, so you can't switch it off in a weak moment. This page has two parts: one for you, and one for the friend.

Part 1: for you, setting up the app

Out of the box, the app already blocks a small built-in list of video and social sites. To follow your friend's list instead (or as well), you link it once:

  1. Ask your friend to keep a list for you, and to send you the link to it. (Part 2 below shows them how, in about five minutes.)
  2. Open Enuvi Browser and tap the menu (⋯), then Settings.
  3. Under Blocklist, paste your friend's link into the box and press Link.

The first time you link a list, it applies immediately. The app checks the list every time it starts.

The rules, in plain words

What changes on the listWhat happens in your app
Your friend adds a siteIt is blocked right away.
Your friend removes a siteIt stays blocked for 24 more hours, then it is unblocked.
The site is added back before the 24 hours are upThe removal is cancelled.
You ask the app to follow a different listIt switches only after 24 hours. You can cancel the request.

There is no button that turns blocking off, and no "unlock for five minutes." The 24-hour wait is on purpose: the urge to watch something is an urge to watch it now, not tomorrow.

If the list can't be reached (you're offline, or the page is down), the app keeps using the last list it had. It never falls back to "block nothing."

Part 2: for the friend who keeps the list

No technical background needed. You are being asked to keep one small file up to date. You don't have to watch anyone or be available. You only act when they ask.

Setting it up (about 5 minutes)

You need a free GitHub account at github.com. The list lives in a "Gist", which is a small public text file.

  1. Go to gist.github.com and sign in.
  2. In Filename, type exactly blocklist.txt.
  3. In the big box, paste a starter list (see below). One site per line. Lines starting with # are notes and are ignored.
  4. Optional but nice: make the first line your name, like this: # owner: Sam. The app then says "This site is blocked. It's on Sam's list," so your friend knows who to ask.
  5. Click Create public gist.
  6. Copy the address of the page you land on. It looks like https://gist.github.com/yourname/1a2b3c...
  7. Send that address to your friend. They paste it into the app, as in Part 1.

Starter lists

You don't have to start from nothing. Open one, copy the lines, and paste them into your Gist. Mix them as you like:

  • Video and streaming
  • Social media
  • News sites
  • Online shopping
  • Gambling and betting (a starting point, not a complete list)
  • Games

These are suggestions. Delete anything your friend genuinely needs, and add what they don't.

Changing the list later

Open your Gist, click Edit, change the lines, and click Update public gist. The app picks up the change the next time it starts.

  • To block something new, add a line. It takes effect straight away.
  • To unblock something, delete its line. It unblocks after 24 hours.

Write sites as plain names: youtube.com, not a full address. You don't need www.: a site also covers all its subdomains.

Things to know

  • Keep the filename blocklist.txt. The app looks for that name.
  • The Gist is public. Anyone with the link can read it. Don't put anything private in it.
  • If you make a mistake, fix it and save again. The app keeps the last good list until it can read a valid one, and an empty or broken file is ignored.
  • You can see what changed. Gists keep a history of every edit.
  • This is a commitment tool, not a lock. Someone determined can open a different browser, or clear the app's data on their phone, which restarts the 24-hour wait. It works best as a promise between friends, which is why your name on the list matters.
